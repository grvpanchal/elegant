# LinkedIn: the bar GreatFrontEnd's pairs series sets

**Source.** CMO report, evidence [E22] (https://www.linkedin.com/company/greatfrontend, fetched 2026-10-17), playbook
"What their best posts have in common", and the `linkedin-carousels` bar in `benzene-standards.yaml`.

**What the report records.**

- GreatFrontEnd's Page has 89,925 followers. Its 10 visible posts were aged 1 to 5 days, and their reactions ranged
  from 11 to 208.
- The openings it quotes include a series: "5 CSS animation pairs that sound similar, but work differently: Regular CSS
  transition vs @starting-style…", "5 React pairs that sound similar, but work differently … Save this before your next
  React interview" and "5 image and media pairs…".
- The report names this series as the model for our terminology carousels: it "maps 1:1 onto our glossary".

**What it does not record.** It gives no per-post reaction count, so we cannot say which post earned the 208. It gives
no character count either. It also could not confirm that any competitor post is a document (carousel), because
company-page HTML does not show the post type. `media` and `text_chars` are left empty on purpose, and `reach` keeps the
range rather than a number for this post.

**The bar for us.**

- Copy the format, not the topic. Open with "N <layer> pairs that sound similar but work differently". Give one pair
  per slide and end with a save-or-practise call to action.
- The reach to beat is the top of that range: 208 reactions on one post, earned from 89,925 followers. Our Page had 2
  followers on 2026-10-17 (analytics snapshot), so for the first twelve weeks the bar is the format, not the number.
- Other LinkedIn reference points from the report: Master.dev has 148,026 followers and 11–31 reactions per post
  [E45]; Frontend Mentor has 34,843 followers and 2–30 [E61]; roadmap.sh has 9,687 followers and 1–5 [E142].
