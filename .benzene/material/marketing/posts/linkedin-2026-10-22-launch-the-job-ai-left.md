If an agent took the typing part of your frontend job, the part that's left is the harness.

Teams are finding this out the expensive way. An agent ships features fast until the codebase is big enough that every change breaks two others. Then they pay senior engineers for a week to cut it back and add the guardrails it never had.

The people they pay are not faster typists. They know three things the agent didn't:

1. Where each piece of code belongs, and what it may depend on.
2. Which rules every file of that kind has to follow.
3. Which check proves a change is done.

All three can be learned, and none of them goes out of date when the next model ships. They are what Elegant is built around: the terms of the UI, Server and State layers, the Agent Skills that encode them, and practice questions that make you write the checks yourself.

If you were a good frontend developer last year, you already know half of this. Elegant names the other half and makes you practise it. Free, no sign-up.

The practice bank is in the first comment, six harness questions included.
